# Construction pilot: Mann's hexapillars as qualifying polyforms (2026-10-27)

## Outcome

No qualifying realization of Mann's H=5 hexapillar was found. A faithful copy does not fit the size limits. The three compact realizations that do fit fail in two different ways: they either tile the plane or cannot form a single corona. Their one-cell neighbourhoods reach at most Hc=1. This is a negative result for direct realization of the hexapillar mechanism under SPEC §1. It is not a statement about other marked constructions.

## Mann's rules (extracted from `refs/mann_2004_heesch.pdf`, Figure 5, Theorem 1)

An n-hexapillar is n regular hexagons in a row, sharing edges. Every bump and nick is the same equilateral triangle on the middle third of an edge, so any bump fits any nick, including on reflected copies:

| Edge of each hexagon | Marking |
| --- | --- |
| both upper edges | nick |
| both lower edges | bump |
| outer edge of the first hexagon | flat |
| outer edge of the last hexagon | nick |

That gives 2n+1 nicks against 2n bumps. The tile can't tile because it is combinatorially unbalanced: in a large patch the surplus nicks cannot all lie on the boundary. Theorem 1: H = 4 for n = 2, 3 and H = 5 for n ≥ 4.

## Realizations tested (`halo/hexapillar.py`)

| Realization | 4-pillar size | Within limits | heesch-sat | Diagnosis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Triangle grid, side-3 hexagons (Mann exact: bump = one unit triangle) | 215 triangles; span far above 29 (body alone 86, `check_hex_chain_budget.py`) | no | not run | over budget |
| Hex grid, 7-cell macro-hexagons (k=2) | 27 cells, spans 5+8 | yes | isohedral (n = 2–5) | single-cell bumps/nicks are too coarse; shapes slip into a tiling |
| Hex grid, 19-cell macro-hexagons (k=3) | 75 cells, spans 11+14 | yes | Hc=0 (n = 2–4) | chiral macro-lattice; see below |
| Hex grid, 37-cell macro-hexagons (k=4) | 147 cells | no (span) | n=2: Hc=0 | as k=3 |
| Triangle grid, side-1 hexagons (bump = whole-edge triangle) | 23 triangles, spans 14+12 | yes (n ≤ 4) | isohedral (n = 2–5) | whole-edge features too coarse |

**How the hex-grid version works:** a macro-hexagon is every cell within hex distance k−1 of a centre. Macro-hexagons tile by the lattice generated by (2k−1, −(k−1)) and its 60° rotations (checked: 37 macro-hexagons, no overlap or gap, for k = 2, 3, 4). Each macro-edge's midpoint is the midpoint of a cell edge between cell a (own) and cell b (neighbour). A bump adds b and a nick removes a, so bump/nick, bump/bump, nick/nick and flat combinations behave like Mann's.

**Why k ≥ 3 gives Hc=0:** with k=3 and three hexagons, the plain chain tiles, top nicks plus bottom bumps (balanced) tile, and adding the end nick gives Hc=0 instantly. Mann's coronas need mirror-image copies (for example, a reflected pillar's flat end meeting ours). The macro-lattice for k ≥ 2 is chiral (tilted relative to the hex grid), so a reflected pillar's macro-hexagons sit on the mirror lattice and cannot mate edge-to-edge with ours. **An achiral alternative doesn't exist in this range:** a search over D6-symmetric unions of orbits that form fundamental domains of the achiral lattices mA2 (m = 5, 7; 11 had no candidates), up to radius 9, found no connected, hole-free macro-tile (`scripts/construction_d6_domains.py`).

**Neighbourhood check:** every valid one-cell edit of the k=2 4- and 5-hexapillars was classified. 4-pillar: 753 variants → 401 Hc=0, 240 Hc=1, 109 isohedral, 3 periodic. 5-pillar: 1,121 → 770 Hc=0, 242 Hc=1, 106 isohedral, 3 periodic (`scripts/construction_hexapillar_sweep.py`). All classifications: `results/construction_hexapillar_20261027.jsonl`.

## What this suggests

- The 200-cell / span-29 limits are tight relative to known marked constructions. Mann's mechanism needs bumps and nicks much smaller than the hexagon edge, plus an achiral substrate, and neither fits.
- Bašić's H=6 figure (six hexagons, eleven triangles and a half triangle) needs a scale-2 triangle grid to be a polyform. That is roughly 190 triangles, but the sparse-coordinate span would be far above 29, so it is not attempted.
- The plausible routes left are (a) new compact markings designed for these limits (RESEARCH_PLAN.md: coding theory + inverse SAT), which is open research, and (b) continued empirical search around certified Hc=4 shapes (the two-edit sweep).
